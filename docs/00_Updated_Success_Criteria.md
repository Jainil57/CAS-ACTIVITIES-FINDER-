Several of the original criteria can't be tested inside the IA. Examples are "participation rates have increased" and "the interface is simple, clean and easy to use". Criteria C and D are therefore written against the refined list below. Replace the list in Criterion A with it so that all five criteria use the same SC numbers.

## Suggested context for Criterion A (GMIS, Jakarta)

State the problem at your own school. At Gandhi Memorial International School (GMIS) in Kemayoran, Jakarta Pusat, CAS opportunities are shared through WhatsApp groups and posters. Students miss them, and organizers can't find volunteers. Jakarta's IBDP schools are spread across the city (Jakarta Pusat, Jakarta Selatan, Tangerang and so on). Students have no shared place to find interschool activities such as beach clean-ups at Ancol, mangrove planting at Angke Kapuk or futsal leagues. Fees are in Rupiah, and deadlines follow Jakarta time (WIB, UTC+7).

## Other changes needed in Criteria A and B (May 2027 rubric)

1. **Computational context.** Your current draft says *Java back end*. The product is now written fully in **JavaScript** (Node.js + Express on the server, plain JavaScript in the browser) with an **SQLite** database. Here is a suggested justification. One language on both client and server means the validation rules and data format (JSON) are shared. SQLite is a single file that needs no separate database server, so the system runs on a school laptop. It also still supports relational constraints (foreign keys, UNIQUE, CHECK).
2. **No client needed.** The 2027 IA doesn't require a formal client, consultation evidence or a "Record of Tasks". Keep the apartment-complex students as the *intended users* who motivate the problem. You can drop the client-meeting tasks or relabel them as user research.
3. **Criterion headings.** The new names are A *Problem specification*, B *Planning*, C *System overview*, D *Development*, E *Evaluation*. Your draft still uses the older names: "A: Planning", "B: Solution Overview".
